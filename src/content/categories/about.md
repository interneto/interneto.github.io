> The name **Interneto** derives from "**Inter**-communicated **Net**works", with the **O** representing closed circular connections.

Started in 2020 by **David7ce** as a curated web directory. Has since grown into a broader resource-discovery site: a **toolbox** (cross-platform package installer + OS/library compatibility tables) and a **blog** (posts/docs) alongside the directory.

## Why Curation

Indexing the whole web is intractable — of an estimated 1–2 billion registered domains, fewer than ~200 million are actively maintained (*Netcraft*, *Majestic Million*). Interneto uses **selective curation**: functional relevance, technical value, signal over noise.

## Historical Context

Earlier manual indexes — **DMOZ**, **Curlie** — struggled with scale and human moderation. Search engines and AI interfaces now dominate discovery, optimizing retrieval over structure. Curated directories still offer: low infrastructure overhead, privacy (no tracking), high signal density, independence from SEO incentives, portability. They function more like **knowledge bases** than search engines.

## Structure vs Discoverability

| Approach      | Strength    | Limitation            |
|---------------|-------------|-----------------------|
| Hierarchical  | Predictable | Rigid                 |
| Tag-based     | Flexible    | Can get noisy         |
| Search-driven | Fast        | Weak global structure |

Interneto: hierarchical categories + tags + static export.

## Related Projects

[AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/), [FMHY](https://fmhy.net/), [Eylenburg](https://eylenburg.github.io/) — these emphasize breadth and community contribution; **Interneto focuses narrower: structured classification, long-term knowledge organization**.

## Perspective

Interneto doesn't index whole web. Treats it as **high-entropy system** — value from selective filtering and structure.

> A smaller, well-structured subset of the web can be more useful than an exhaustive but unstructured whole.
